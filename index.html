I’m Kaleab. I’m from Ethiopia, and I’m a senior at Vanderbilt University, double majoring in computer science and mathematics.

I work where machine learning, mathematics and neuroscience meet. In Vanderbilt’s Attention-Circuits-Control Lab, where I’ve been since 2023, I decode behavior from primate neural recordings. I also use topological data analysis to study brain imaging and language-model features. Over two summers as an ML Foundations intern at Dropbox, I built infrastructure for serving and evaluating language models. I also TA algorithms courses.

Long term, I want to do a PhD in AI/ML theory, on the mathematical foundations of machine learning. Along the way, when I learn something the hard way, I try to write it up so the next person doesn’t have to.

The research page has the details, and the CV has the short version.

Decoding trial outcomes from macaque neural activity

paper in progress

Predicting whether a trial was correct or an error from multiunit activity in the anterior cingulate cortex, prefrontal cortex and caudate, recorded with silicon probes while monkeys did a feature-learning task.

in progress

How monkeys learn which visual feature (color, pattern or shape) is rewarded, and how they move from exploring to exploiting.
